Flame in Freefall

By: Team Mevidakasha

Summary

What did you develop?

    We built a SI-powered dashboard that takes NASA's microgravity combustion data and turns it into clear fire safety tips. We analyzed real data from NASA’s PSI database and organized the most important information, like the fuel type, oxygen level, pressure, airflow, and burn time. We then used the learning from the analysis dataset and created a combustion risk scoring system for these three fuel types: Solid, Liquid, and Gas.

How does it address the challenge?

    The challenge asked us to make a dashboard that summarizes, ranks, and interprets NASA’s fire experiments to help keep people safe in space. Our dashboard does that by:
    The dashboard summarizes each experiment's goal, variables, and results in simple and easy-to-read words.


    1. Summarizing each experiment's goal, variables, and results in a simple and easy-to-read format.

    2. It ranks how risky each test is based on real data from previous NASA experiments. We assess a combustion rating of Low, Medium, or High Risk, so that people can analyze what type of conditions are most prone to cause fires and eventually dangerous situations.

      3. It provides the range of environmental conditions (eg: O2 flow, temperatures, burn times) for each combustion rating so that future experiments can be designed appropriately based on the risk appetite.

Why is it important?

    Fire is one of the biggest dangers in space, because astronauts can’t escape it and there is nobody to save them. In space, fires behave very differently compared to fires on Earth due to gravity. Airflow from fans can make fires spread rapidly, and some fires in space keep on burning even though the flame is not visible. This is why our dashboard turns NASA’s complex fire data into clear and easy-to-understand risk categories, helping people in space safely identify flames in space before they can spread, keeping them safe.

Project Details

Different types of fuels

  • Solid Fuels (Experiments: BASS-II, PSI-25): scored by how much oxygen was used and how much carbon dioxide (CO₂) and carbon monoxide (CO) were produced. Solid fuel types covered are PMMA, NOMEX, spherical solids, SIBAL, rod, and wax candle.

  • Liquid Fuels (Experiments: FLEX, PSI-69): scored by how much oxygen was used and how much carbon dioxide (CO₂) and carbon monoxide (CO) were produced. Liquid fuel types covered: heptane and methanol.

  • Gaseous Fuels (CFI-G, PSI-159): scored by burn time and peak burner temperature - Gaseous fuel types covered are Ethane, Butane, & Propane

    C. Using the model developed, we created a user-friendly tool to assess combustion risk, where given a fuel type and material, sliders can help identify ranges of other parameters which will decide the risk level. For example;
  • For gas fuel: a burn time over 51 seconds with a peak burner temperature over 166 °C is high risk. If the peak burner temperature stays below 166 °C, the risk drops to moderate. If the burn time is also under 28 seconds, the risk drops to low.

  • For solid fuel: if the oxygen goes down while CO₂ and carbon monoxide both go up, it is high risk. If carbon monoxide stays about the same (a rise of 24 ppm or less), the risk drops to moderate. If all three gases barely change, the risk is low.

  • For liquid fuel: a burn time over 16 seconds with the fuel droplet shrinking more than 29% before the flame goes out is high risk. If the droplet shrinks less than 29%, the risk drops to moderate. If the burn time is also under 10 seconds, the risk drops to low.

    Methodology and sources

    Gas-fuel combustion classification uses Burn Time and Peak Temperature. Values are ranked separately within each gas fuel and divided into three groups (tertiles). Each measurement receives a score of 1, 2, or 3. The two scores are averaged to determine combustion classification.

    Liquid-fuel combustion classification uses Burn Time and Diameter Reduction. Burn Time is divided into tertiles separately for each liquid fuel. Diameter Reduction is calculated as: (Initial Diameter - Extinction Diameter) / Initial Diameter × 100. Diameter Reduction is also divided into three tertiles separately for each liquid fuel. The liquid score is the average of the Burn Time Score and Diameter Reduction Score.

    Identical values are never separated across tertiles. If a tied group reaches or crosses a tertile boundary, the entire group is assigned to the higher tertile. This prevents overlapping minimum and maximum ranges.

    Solid-fuel combustion classification continues to use directional changes in the available experimental measurements. Gas and liquid tertile scoring does not change the solid-fuel classification logic.

    Flammability classification is a separate project-defined rule using final O2 and flow measurements. These rules should not be treated as universal fire-safety thresholds.
